Best tide for tide pooling: how low, when, and how to find minus tides

By Mohamed Elatabany, developer of SeaWindow · Updated November 2026

The best tide for tide pooling is a minus tide, a low tide that falls below zero on a NOAA tide table, reached in daylight. Arrive at least an hour before the predicted low and head back no later than an hour after it, as the US National Park Service advises at Point Reyes, and watch the waves the whole time.

What a minus tide is

Heights on a US tide table are measured from Mean Lower Low Water (MLLW), which NOAA defines as the average of the lower low water of each tidal day. Because it is an average, some lows fall below it. Those show as negative numbers, such as −1.5 ft, and are called minus tides. The lower the number, the more of the rocky shore is uncovered.

The Park Service at Point Reyes puts it simply: “The best time to go tidepooling is during a minus low tide,” and “not all low tides are minus tides”. Some lows are too high to reach the pools at all. In the UK and other places that use chart datum, close to the lowest astronomical tide, negative heights are rare; look for the lowest lows of the month instead.

The two hours around low tide

The water keeps falling until the predicted low and starts rising right after. The Point Reyes advice is to “be at the tidepool area at least an hour before low tide so that you have plenty of time to explore safely while the water is receding”, and to return no later than an hour after the tide has begun to rise. That gives about two hours with the lowest water in the middle. The tide height calculator shows how fast the water comes back on either side of the low.

Daylight: winter afternoons, summer mornings

On the US West Coast the lowest tides of the month shift through the day with the seasons. Cabrillo National Monument in San Diego says the ideal time to visit its tide pools is fall and winter, “when low tides coincide with park hours”, while in spring and summer the tide often covers the pools during open hours. The Monterey predictions below show the same pattern: winter minus tides in the afternoon and evening, summer ones in the early morning.

Worked example: Monterey, December 2026

NOAA’s low tide predictions for station 9413450 (Monterey, California), retrieved from the NOAA CO-OPS data service on November 19, 2026, in feet above MLLW and Pacific Standard Time. Sunset times are from the US Naval Observatory for the station’s position. December 2026 has 18 minus tides, all between 1:58 PM and 8:10 PM. Eight come before sunset.   • The easier day may be December 22: −1.4 ft at 3:31 PM, and the whole window, 2:31 to 4:31 PM, is in daylight.

In June 2027 the pattern flips. The lowest tides come in the early morning, from −1.4 ft at 5:12 AM on June 4, before the 5:50 AM sunrise, to −1.8 ft at 6:47 AM on June 6, after it (daylight saving time).

Safety

  • Sneaker waves. At Point Reyes, the Park Service warns that tidepoolers at spots facing the open ocean “have lost their lives” when struck by sneaker waves. “Never turn your back to the ocean.”
  • The rising tide. Know your way back before the water turns, and leave on time.
  • Footing. NOAA advises stepping on bare rock, which is less slippery than rock covered in algae. Cabrillo recommends closed-toe shoes with good grip and keeping small children close.
  • Weather. NOAA notes that onshore winds can pile water up and virtually eliminate low tide exposures, so a minus tide on paper may not appear.

Etiquette

NOAA’s tide pool tips: leave animals and plants where they are, put any rock you lift back as you found it, and do not collect: it is illegal in many areas. The Park Service adds: stand and walk on bare rock or sand, not on mussels, barnacles or seaweed, and keep your distance from seals and sea lions. If you cannot hide the animal behind your thumb at arm’s length, you are too close.

FAQ

What is a minus tide?

A low tide predicted below zero on the tide table. In the US, zero is Mean Lower Low Water, so a −1.5 ft tide is 1.5 ft below the average of the lowest daily tides.

How low does the tide need to be for tide pooling?

Lower is better. The National Park Service recommends minus tides; at Monterey in December 2026, the lowest daylight tides reach −1.4 to −1.7 ft.

How long before low tide should I arrive?

At least an hour before the predicted low, and leave no later than an hour after it, as the Park Service advises at Point Reyes.

Why are the best low tides in the afternoon in winter?

On the US West Coast the lowest tides fall in the afternoon and evening in winter and the early morning in summer, as the Monterey predictions show. Cabrillo National Monument says its fall and winter low tides coincide with park hours.

Can I take shells or animals from tide pools?

Leave them. NOAA says collecting intertidal species is illegal in many areas.
