Spring tides are the larger tides around each new and full moon, when the sun and moon pull in line; neap tides are the smaller tides around the first and last quarter, when they pull at right angles. Each comes twice a lunar month, about a week apart, and at many places the largest tides arrive a day or two after the new or full moon.

Why the moon’s phase changes the range

NOAA says solar tides are about half as large as lunar tides. At new and full moon the sun, moon and Earth are nearly in line, and the sun’s pull adds to the moon’s: high tides are higher and low tides lower. These are spring tides, from the tide “springing forth”, not the season. About seven days later, at the quarter moons, the sun and moon are at right angles, the solar tide partly cancels the lunar one, and the tides are the moderate neap tides.

The moon’s distance matters too. NOAA notes that at perigee, the moon’s closest point about once every 28 days, the tide range is larger than average, and about 14 days later, at apogee, smaller. When perigee lines up with a new or full moon, six to eight times a year, you get the extra-large perigean spring tides often called king tides.

The lag after new and full moon

The biggest tides often arrive after the new or full moon, not at it. NOAA’s glossary calls the delay the age of phase inequality and gives a formula from a station’s harmonic constants: 0.984 × (S2 phase − M2 phase) hours. With NOAA’s published constants (Greenwich phases), it works out at about 36 hours at Boston, 26 hours at Charleston and only 8 hours at San Francisco.

At San Francisco, where the lag is about 8 hours, NOAA’s predictions for the same month put the largest daily range, 9.1 ft, on December 24, the day after the full moon by local time, and the smallest, 3.7 ft, on December 17.

What the moon phase cannot tell you

  • The exact size. The phase, the moon’s distance and its declination all change the range. Read the prediction for your station rather than the calendar.
  • Diurnal coasts. NOAA’s glossary says the spring range is “of no practical significance” where the tide is mainly diurnal, as in many parts of the Gulf of Mexico.
  • Weather. Wind and air pressure can add to a spring tide or hold it back. SeaWindow is a planning aid, not a navigation or safety tool.

FAQ

Do spring tides only happen in spring?

No. The name comes from the tide “springing forth”. Spring tides come twice a month, all year.

How long after a full moon is the biggest tide?

It depends on the station. From NOAA’s harmonic constants the lag is about 36 hours at Boston, 26 hours at Charleston and 8 hours at San Francisco.

How much bigger are spring tides than neap tides?

It varies by place and month. At Boston, NOAA predicts a daily range of 7.8 ft on December 17, 2026 and 13.5 ft on December 25.

Are tidal currents stronger at spring tides?

Yes. NOAA calls the strong currents at new and full moon spring currents, and the weak ones at the quarters neap currents.

Is a king tide a spring tide?

Yes, an extra-large one: usually a perigean spring tide, when a new or full moon coincides with the moon’s closest approach.
