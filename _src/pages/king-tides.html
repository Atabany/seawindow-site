What makes a king tide

Tides grow at every new and full moon, when the sun’s pull adds to the moon’s; these are spring tides. NOAA explains that the moon also reaches its closest point to Earth, perigee, about once every 28 days, when the tide range is larger than average. When a new or full moon coincides closely with perigee, typically six to eight times a year, the result is a perigean spring tide.

NOAA calls “king tide” a popular, non-scientific term for exceptionally high tides; the California Coastal Commission describes them as the year’s highest high tides, one to two feet higher than average high tides. They are in every tide table months ahead. Weather on the day decides whether the water goes higher still.

Why they come in winter on the West Coast

NOAA’s tide FAQ says that along coastlines like the U.S. West Coast, the range of perigean spring tides is largest near the summer and winter solstices, because of the added effect of the sun’s declination. Earth is also closest to the sun around January 2, which NOAA notes enhances tidal ranges. On the East and Gulf Coasts, NOAA adds, the seasonal peak in mean sea level comes in late summer and early fall, and raises perigean spring tides then.

At San Francisco this season the king tides also fall in daylight: the six highest predicted high tides between November 2026 and February 2027 all come between 9:39 AM and 11:30 AM.

King tides and high tide flooding

NOAA says perigean spring tides can exceed the average daily highest tide (Mean Higher High Water, MHHW) by 0.5 ft or more, raising the local flood risk, but in most places flooding needs another factor, such as wind or currents, on top.

NOAA rates high tide flooding, also called sunny day or nuisance flooding, as minor at 1.8 ft (0.55 m) above average high tide, moderate at 2.8 ft (0.85 m) and major at 3.9 ft (1.20 m), and says it is now twice as frequent in U.S. coastal communities as 20 years ago. The Coastal Commission notes that king tides are not caused by climate change but preview everyday water levels as the sea rises.

What the prediction cannot tell you

  • Storm surge and waves. Tide predictions leave out weather, and SeaWindow does not show storm surge. The Coastal Commission’s advice for king tide photographers: stay back from big waves, never turn your back on the ocean, and heed closures and warnings.
  • How deep a street floods. That depends on the height of the land and drainage, not just the tide.
  • Safety. A tide prediction is a planning aid, not a navigation or safety tool. Check official coastal flood and marine warnings.

FAQ

Is a king tide the same as a spring tide?

Not quite. Every new and full moon brings spring tides. King tide is the popular name for the highest of them, usually perigean spring tides, when a new or full moon is close to the moon’s perigee.

When are the next king tides in California?

November 24–26 and December 23–25, 2026, and January 21–22, 2027, according to the California King Tides Project.

Why are king tides bigger in winter on the West Coast?

NOAA says West Coast perigean spring tides have their largest range near the solstices, because of the sun’s declination, and Earth is closest to the sun around January 2.

How much higher is a king tide than a normal high tide?

The California Coastal Commission says one to two feet higher than average high tides. At San Francisco, the December 24, 2026 high of 7.2 ft is 1.4 ft above the station’s Mean Higher High Water.
