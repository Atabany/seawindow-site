On most coasts high tide comes twice a day, about 12 hours 25 minutes apart, and arrives about 50 minutes later each day than the day before, because the moon returns over the same spot every 24 hours and 50 minutes. Today’s high tide is a prediction for a particular station, so look it up for the station nearest you rather than reusing yesterday’s time.

The lunar day: 24 hours 50 minutes

NOAA explains that a lunar day, also called a tidal day, is the time it takes a spot on Earth to rotate from directly under the moon back to directly under it. It lasts 24 hours and 50 minutes, because the moon moves along its orbit in the same direction that Earth turns, so Earth needs an extra 50 minutes to catch up. NOAA’s glossary gives the mean lunar day as 24.84 solar hours.

Earth turns through two tidal bulges each lunar day, so most coasts get two highs and two lows every 24 hours 50 minutes. NOAA puts high tides 12 hours 25 minutes apart, with about 6 hours 12.5 minutes from high to low. Each day’s tides therefore run about 50 minutes behind the previous day’s, and over two weeks a morning high moves around to the evening.

Two highs a day, one, or two unequal ones

  • Semidiurnal. Two highs and two lows of about the same height each lunar day. NOAA says this is typical of the US East Coast.
  • Mixed semidiurnal. Two highs and two lows of different heights: a higher high and a lower high. NOAA says the US West Coast tends to have mixed tides. When someone asks “when is high tide?” there, the answer may be two times with very different heights.
  • Diurnal. One high and one low each lunar day, as in many parts of the Gulf of Mexico.

How to read today’s high tide for your coast

  1. Pick the right station. Use the nearest station on the same stretch of water. Inside a bay the tide can arrive later than at the entrance; see why tide times differ.
  2. Check the clock. Make sure the times are local and include daylight saving (summer time in the UK).
  3. Find both highs. On mixed coasts, note which is the higher high. On some days there is only one high before midnight.
  4. Read the height too. A high of 9.8 ft and one of 11.8 ft cover very different amounts of beach. The tide chart guide explains the datum behind the number.
  5. Allow for weather. Wind and air pressure move the real water away from the prediction.

In the UK and Australia people usually call these tide times, and tide tables list each day’s high and low water.

What a high tide time cannot tell you

  • Weather. NOAA notes that onshore wind and low pressure can bring tides higher than predicted.
  • Your exact spot. The prediction belongs to the station; SeaWindow shows its distance so you can judge.

FAQ

How many high tides are there in a day?

Usually two, about 12 hours 25 minutes apart. Some coasts, such as many parts of the Gulf of Mexico, have one. Because the tide cycle runs on a 24-hour 50-minute lunar day, some calendar days have only one high.

Why is high tide about 50 minutes later each day?

A lunar day lasts 24 hours 50 minutes, so Earth takes about 50 extra minutes each day to bring the same spot back under the moon.

Is high tide at the same time everywhere?

No. Each station has its own times. In San Francisco Bay, NOAA predicts highs at Coyote Point Marina 42 minutes after the Golden Gate.

Can there be only one high tide in a day?

Yes. In Boston on November 26, 2026, NOAA predicts one high, at 11:49 AM; the next is just after midnight. On diurnal coasts one high a day is normal.
