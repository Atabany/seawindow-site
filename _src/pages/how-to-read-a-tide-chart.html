A tide chart is a curve of predicted water height over time: the peaks are high tides, the dips are low tides, and every height is measured from a reference level called the datum. Read the time along the bottom, the height up the side, and check the station and datum before comparing the number with anything else.

The four parts of a tide chart

  1. Time. The horizontal axis is the clock, normally the local time of the station. Check whether daylight saving time applies.
  2. Height. The vertical axis is the predicted water level in feet or metres. It is not water depth: it is the height of the sea surface above the datum.
  3. Highs and lows. Each peak is a high tide and each trough a low tide. Between them the water is rising (a flood tide) or falling (an ebb tide). NOAA explains that most coasts get two highs and two lows in a lunar day of 24 hours and 50 minutes, which is why tide times move later from one day to the next.
  4. Station and datum. A prediction belongs to one place. Heights from a distant station, or from a different datum, can differ from your beach.

What the datum means

Zero on a tide chart is a chosen reference level, not the sea floor. In the United States NOAA predictions use Mean Lower Low Water (MLLW), which NOAA defines as “the average of the lower low water height of each tidal day” over the National Tidal Datum Epoch. Because MLLW is an average of the lowest daily tides, some lows fall below it. Those show as negative numbers, often called minus tides, and they expose more shoreline than usual.

Outside the United States the reference can be a different chart datum, and some sources use Mean Sea Level, so the same moment can carry different numbers. At San Francisco, NOAA’s datum sheet puts Mean Sea Level 3.12 ft above MLLW and Mean Higher High Water 5.84 ft above MLLW (1983–2001 epoch). Subtract 3.12 ft from an MLLW height to get the height relative to mean sea level.

Worked example: San Francisco, 5 November 2026

These are NOAA’s predictions for station 9414290 (San Francisco, Golden Gate), in local time, retrieved from the NOAA CO-OPS data service on 4 November 2026. Feet and metres are heights above MLLW (1 ft is exactly 0.3048 m). “vs MSL” is the same height relative to mean sea level.

9:03 PMHigh4.7 ft1.43 m1.6 ft
  • Range. The water rises 5.1 ft from the early low to the morning high over 6 hours 48 minutes, then falls 5.2 ft by mid-afternoon.
  • Unequal highs. The morning high is 1.2 ft higher than the evening high. NOAA calls this a mixed semidiurnal tide and notes it is typical of the U.S. West Coast.
  • Timing. The two highs are 12 hours 28 minutes apart, close to NOAA’s average of 12 hours 25 minutes.
  • Datum check. The “0.7 ft” low is 2.4 ft below mean sea level. Both numbers are right; they use different zeros.

Compare 24 December 2026, within a day of the full moon (US Naval Observatory). NOAA predicts a high of 7.2 ft (2.20 m) at 10:37 AM and a low of −1.8 ft (−0.56 m) at 5:42 PM: a 9.1 ft fall, about 3.8 ft more than the largest swing on 5 November. NOAA explains that the larger spring tides come around new and full moon, and the moderate neap tides when the sun and moon are at right angles.

What a tide chart cannot tell you

  • Weather. Predictions are astronomical. NOAA notes that onshore wind can pile water up, offshore wind can exaggerate low tides, and low pressure can raise levels above the prediction.
  • Your exact spot. Bays, harbours and rivers can differ from the nearest station. SeaWindow shows the station distance so you can judge this.
  • Model estimates. Where no station is near, SeaWindow estimates the tide from a global model and says so. Times and heights can be off, especially inside bays and rivers.
  • Safety. A tide chart is not a navigation or safety tool, and neither is SeaWindow. Check official marine forecasts, warnings and local conditions before going on or near the water.

FAQ

What does a negative tide mean?

The water is predicted to fall below the datum. With NOAA’s MLLW datum, a reading such as −1.8 ft means 1.8 ft lower than the average of the lowest daily tides.

Why are the two high tides different heights?

Many coasts, including the U.S. West Coast, have a mixed semidiurnal tide, where the two daily highs and lows differ in height.

Why doesn’t the water at my beach match the chart?

The chart predicts the tide at one station without weather. Distance from the station, local geography, wind and air pressure all change what you see.

Is a tide chart the same as a tide table?

A tide table lists the times and heights of highs and lows. The curve joins those points. NOAA reserves “tide chart” for maps of a bay, but most apps use it for the curve.

How do I switch SeaWindow between feet and metres?
